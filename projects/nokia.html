Internship · Optical communications

Optical test engineering at Nokia

Optical characterization, laboratory automation and measurement analysis for optical communications hardware.

Characterizing wavelength-selective switches

A wavelength-selective switch routes optical channels to different output ports. Characterization involves measuring how much power reaches the selected port and how much leaks to other ports. Optical path loss and detector limits can distort both measurements, so the setup has to be calibrated before its readings can be attributed to the device.

I built and operated test setups using tunable lasers, optical spectrum analyzers, power meters, and oscilloscope and photodiode acquisition. I calibrated optical path loss and troubleshot setup faults. Spectral measurements show how power varies with wavelength, while the photodiode and oscilloscope show how the signal changes over time.

Automation and measurement quality

I migrated MATLAB test routines to modular Python for C- and L-band testing, covering instrument control, data acquisition and processing. I also implemented detection of photodiode saturation and automated adjustment of a variable optical attenuator. This protects the measurement from clipping, which can flatten peaks and corrupt the captured response.

I developed FFT-based filtering to suppress baseline noise in oscilloscope traces and compared optical signal-to-noise ratio estimation methods using simulated noisy spectra. I also automated parts of test reporting so engineers could review the results alongside the underlying measurements.
